Thesis pillars

  • Demand must sustain the acquired scaleRevenue remains broad and durable after the acquisition step-up rather than depending on a short program cycle or easy comparison.
  • GAAP margins must normalizeGross and operating margins recover as purchase accounting and integration costs normalize, proving that scale creates operating leverage.
  • Cash conversion must become sustainably positiveQuarterly cash improvement converts the negative FY2026 trailing result into repeatable free cash flow rather than a single favorable quarter.
  • Acquired growth must preserve per-share economicsDebt and diluted shares stabilize so integration benefits accrue to each share rather than being absorbed by financing and dilution.

Catalysts

  • Integration and control proofClean control remediation, positive trailing free cash flow and improving GAAP margins would demonstrate that acquired scale is converting into durable economics.
  • Attributable organic demandA quantified organic-versus-acquired revenue bridge with sustained program breadth would raise confidence in the demand pillar.

Risks

  • Integration and controlsMaterial weaknesses, acquisition-accounting complexity and execution risk can delay or obscure economic conversion.
  • Cash conversionNegative trailing free cash flow can persist despite reported revenue growth and a positive single quarter.
  • Dilution and financingA much larger diluted share base and higher debt can prevent platform growth from reaching owners on a per-share basis.
