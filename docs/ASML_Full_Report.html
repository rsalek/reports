Company and Business Overview

ASML supplies lithography systems, software, upgrades, and installed-base services used in advanced semiconductor production. Its economic role is to help customers print increasingly complex chip patterns at commercial yields. The business combines high-value system deliveries with recurring service and field-option revenue from an expanding installed base.

System delivery, customer acceptance, supplier capacity, inventory, and fab schedules can move revenue and cash between periods. Customer concentration, export policy, and product-ramp execution therefore remain material even when end demand is strong. The Q2 operating and balance-sheet baseline is drawn from ASML's public Q2 2026 results, published 2026-07-15.

Thesis and Variant View

The accepted thesis has three pillars: AI-related Logic and Memory investment is converting into stronger system demand, sequential growth in Installed Base Management sales reinforces recurring economics, and strong margins can support attractive long-duration cash generation if working capital normalizes. Q2 net sales rose to EUR 9.326 billion from EUR 8.767 billion in Q1, new-system shipments rose to 86 from 67, and Installed Base Management sales rose to EUR 2.762 billion from EUR 2.488 billion.

The variant question is not whether advanced-node and AI demand exists; the accepted Q2 evidence is supportive. The question is whether ASML can convert that demand into sustainable FCFF quickly enough to justify the dated market price. The reverse DCF implies approximately 20.33% annual FCFF growth, so the public market anchor already assumes a demanding execution path.

The thesis strengthens if Q3 delivery meets the accepted EUR 11 billion sales and 55% gross-margin floors and H2 working-capital conversion turns positive. It weakens if guidance, margins, cash conversion, or lithography-roadmap execution deteriorate.

Business Quality and Moat

ASML's moat rests on the integrated performance of optics, light sources, mechatronics, computational lithography, metrology, field service, and accumulated customer process knowledge. EUV and High-NA leadership make the company a critical production bottleneck, while installed-base service activity adds recurring economics and operational familiarity.

The accepted Q2 evidence supports high business quality: gross margin improved to 54%, quarterly net income was EUR 2.918 billion, and the full-year gross-margin guidance floor remained 54%. The counter-evidence is cash conversion. H1 operating cash flow was negative EUR 482.5 million while inventory reached EUR 11.740 billion. Persistent divergence between profit and cash would weaken the underwriting even if reported margins remain strong.

Growth Drivers

  • AI and EUV demand: AI-related Logic and Memory investment can increase leading-edge capacity and lithography intensity. The next proof is conversion into system deliveries and recognized sales.
  • Installed-base monetization: Service and field-option sales can expand with the active fleet. Q2 Installed Base Management sales of EUR 2.762 billion provide the accepted baseline.
  • Low-NA EUV and DUV capacity: Planned additions can convert customer commitments into 2027-2028 deliveries if supplier and factory execution remain on schedule.
  • High-NA qualification: Successful customer qualification can extend ASML's technology leadership, but timing, throughput, and quality remain execution tests.
  • Margin and working-capital conversion: The growth case becomes more valuable when strong gross margin is accompanied by positive operating cash flow and controlled inventory.

The growth case strengthens only when demand conversion, margin durability, installed-base expansion, and cash conversion improve together. System growth without those companion signals would add scale without fully proving earnings quality.

Monitoring Plan

Review the public operating evidence at each quarterly result and answer these questions:

  1. Did quarterly sales and system deliveries remain consistent with the accepted FY2026 and Q3 guidance floors?
  2. Did gross margin remain at or above 54% and move toward the 55% confirmation gate?
  3. Did Installed Base Management sales continue to grow sequentially?
  4. Did H2 operating cash flow turn positive while inventory and liquidity remained controlled?
  5. Did export policy, customer acceptance, or High-NA/low-NA execution materially change the accessible demand or delivery path?

Refresh the FCFF scenarios only after material changes in operating evidence, discount rates, terminal growth, net debt, share count, currency translation, or the official-exchange price. A lower price improves the entry setup only while the operating thesis remains intact.
