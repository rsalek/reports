Decision Snapshot

ASML remains an exceptional semiconductor-equipment franchise, but the security offers little margin of safety at the dated USD 1,775.64 price. The locked FCFF framework produces USD 479.68 bear, USD 1,295.03 base, and USD 1,858.81 bull monitoring values, with a USD 1,232.14 probability-weighted result. The operating thesis remains supportive; the entry stance remains disciplined because the official price is above both base and probability-weighted value and close to the bull case. The operating baseline comes from ASML's Q2 2026 financial results, dated 2026-07-15, and the price reference comes from the Nasdaq ASML historical record, dated 2026-07-14.

Company and Business Overview

ASML supplies lithography systems, software, upgrades, and installed-base services used in advanced semiconductor production. Its moat comes from the integrated performance of optics, light sources, mechatronics, computational lithography, metrology, field service, and customer process knowledge. EUV and High-NA leadership make ASML a critical production bottleneck, while the installed base creates recurring service and field-option economics.

The model is highly profitable but not capital-light. System delivery, customer acceptance, supplier capacity, inventory, and fab schedules can move revenue and cash between periods. Customer concentration and export controls therefore remain material even when end demand is strong.

Thesis and Variant View

The thesis has three pillars: durable EUV leadership, expanding installed-base monetization, and rising lithography intensity from AI-related logic and memory investment. Q2 results supported all three: quarterly sales increased to EUR 9.33 billion, new-system shipments rose to 86, and installed-base management sales reached EUR 2.92 billion. ASML's Q2 release also raised the FY2026 sales floor to EUR 43 billion.

The variant question is not whether AI demand exists; that is already visible. The question is whether ASML can convert demand into sustainable free cash flow quickly enough to justify the current price. The reverse DCF requires approximately 20.3% annual FCFF growth over five years, so the market already assumes a demanding execution path.

Business Quality and Moat

ASML's technology and service ecosystem supports a strong moat, pricing power, and high returns. Q2 gross margin improved to 54%, while the full-year guidance floor is 54% and the Q3 floor is 55%. The balance sheet remains sound, with a 1.33 current ratio and debt just below EUR 2.0 billion.

The main quality watch is cash conversion. H1 operating cash flow was negative EUR 482.5 million while inventory reached EUR 11.74 billion. This may reflect working-capital and customer-acceptance timing, but persistent divergence between profit and cash would weaken the underwriting.

Growth Drivers

The principal drivers are AI-related leading-edge capacity, EUV layer growth, High-NA qualification, installed-base service expansion, and planned low-NA EUV and DUV immersion capacity additions for 2027–2028. Near-term proof requires Q3 sales at or above EUR 11 billion, gross margin at or above 55%, and visible H2 working-capital normalization.

Risks, Catalysts, and Falsifiers

Key risks are structural export restrictions, customer-capex delays, supplier or capacity constraints, High-NA qualification setbacks, working-capital absorption, and valuation compression. Catalysts are delivery against the raised FY2026 range, Q3 margin execution, H2 cash conversion, and successful EUV capacity expansion.

The thesis requires re-underwriting if FY2026 guidance falls below EUR 43 billion, gross margin falls below 53% without a temporary mix explanation, operating cash flow fails to recover in H2, export rules structurally reduce addressable demand, or ASML loses EUV/High-NA roadmap leadership.

Monitoring Plan

Review quarterly revenue, system shipments, installed-base sales, gross margin, operating cash flow, inventory, liquidity, and guidance. Review immediately on material export-control changes, customer qualification delays, or a High-NA execution setback. A lower price is an entry signal only while the operating thesis remains intact.
