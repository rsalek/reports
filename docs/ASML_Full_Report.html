Exceptional lithography economics, demanding valuation

ASML remains a Phase 4 technology compounder whose EUV position, installed-base service engine, and exposure to AI-related Logic and Memory investment support a high-quality operating thesis. The security thesis is more constrained: the official USD 1,775.64 reference price sits above the base and probability-weighted FCFF values and close to the bull case.

Decision snapshot

Q2 2026 evidence strengthened the operating case. Quarterly sales rose to €9.326 billion from €8.767 billion in Q1, gross margin improved to 54% from 53%, new lithography-system shipments rose to 86 from 67, and installed-base management sales increased to €2.762 billion from €2.488 billion. Management's Q3 floor of €11 billion revenue and 55% gross margin creates a clear, near-term proof point.

The valuation gate does not clear as easily. The preliminary FCFF model produces a USD 1,295.03 base value and USD 1,232.14 probability-weighted value against the dated official USD 1,775.64 close. Even the USD 1,858.81 bull value offers limited headroom. The public research stance is therefore to require either a lower entry price or higher sustainable free cash flow before improving the security posture.

Decision gate: operating quality is not the same as security value. The dashboard should be refreshed if ASML's cash-flow normalization, discount rate, terminal growth, net debt, share count, or currency translation changes materially.

Why the company thesis remains strong

ASML occupies a critical position in advanced semiconductor manufacturing. AI-related investment increases demand for leading-edge Logic and Memory capacity, while the complexity of EUV systems, customer qualification, and the installed service network reinforce a durable competitive position. The Q2 acceleration in systems shipped and revenue is consistent with demand converting into deliveries rather than remaining only a long-duration narrative.

The installed-base business adds an important recurring component. Service and field-option sales grew 11.0% sequentially to €2.762 billion. That stream should expand as the active fleet grows and customers pursue productivity upgrades, reducing reliance on a single quarter's system shipment mix.

What the KPI evidence says

  • Revenue: Q2 sales were €9.326 billion, up 6.4% sequentially, but still below the proposed €11 billion confirmation threshold that matches management's Q3 floor.
  • Gross margin: Q2 reached 54%, up one percentage point sequentially. The next confirmation level is 55%.
  • Systems: new lithography-system shipments rose to 86 from 67, supporting the demand-conversion thesis.
  • Installed base: management sales rose to €2.762 billion, reinforcing the recurring service flywheel.
  • Full-year guide: the public handoff records a 2026 revenue floor of €43 billion and gross-margin floor of 54%.
  • Cash conversion: H1 operating cash flow was negative €482.5 million despite strong profitability, making working-capital release the main quality watch.
  • Liquidity: the current ratio was 1.33, inventory was €11.740 billion, and total debt was €1.984 billion at the Q2 balance-sheet date.

Catalysts, risks, and falsifiers

Constructive evidence

  • Q3 sales meet or exceed €11 billion and gross margin reaches at least 55%.
  • Low-NA EUV and DUV immersion capacity additions convert customer commitments into 2027-2028 deliveries.
  • Installed-base management sales continue to grow with fleet expansion and field upgrades.
  • H2 working-capital release restores positive operating cash flow while preserving net-cash strength.

Escalation evidence

  • Export restrictions or geopolitical policy limit system or service access despite strong end demand.
  • Gross margin falls below the full-year floor without a clearly temporary mix or ramp explanation.
  • Working-capital absorption persists and inventory rises without matching delivery conversion.
  • Capacity or High-NA/low-NA ramps miss customer throughput, timing, or quality requirements.
